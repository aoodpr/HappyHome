# Site photos: observations log

These are the owner's site photos, taken on a Google Pixel 10 Pro with the wide lens. The photos themselves are not in the repo; these are the notes taken from them.
Wide-lens rule: verticals near the frame edges lean and room depth is exaggerated (about 120° field of view, with barrel distortion at the corners). So proportions are read from features near the centre of each frame, and from known sizes as references:

| Reference | Size |
|---|---|
| Block | 400 × 200 mm |
| Wall tile | 600 × 1200 mm |
| Door leaf | ~2.1 m |
| Scaffold frame | 1.7 × 1.2 m |
| Socket plate | 86 mm |

## Batch 1–2: ground-floor kitchen, dining and lift
- **Kitchen ceiling:** flat, pink (fire-rated) gypsum board, flush, with no tray or cornice. The other main rooms have tray ceilings.
- **Kitchen wall services:**
  - Sockets in a row at about 1.1 m (counter height) on two walls.
  - A grey vent pipe drops from the ceiling on the north wall.
  - Water and drain points sit low on the side wall (sink).
- **Dining → kitchen:** a wide, plain, cased opening (no door yet), about 1.8 m wide and full height to about 2.9 m.
- **Kitchen → outside:** a large opening (about 1.8 × 2.6 m) to the side and service yard. Beyond it are the grey rendered boundary wall and a neighbour villa.
  - A 2 × 3 switch / socket block sits beside it.
- **Kitchen store / pantry recess:** tall and narrow (about 0.9 m wide), with no ceiling, so the concrete soffit and conduits show at slab level.
- **Dining / hall ceiling:** a stepped tray (one drop of about 150–200 mm) with a moulded cornice in the step and a shadow-gap reveal. The coffer edges run diagonally in the photos only because of the wide lens.
- **Lift landing (ground floor):** the lift shaft is blockwork. Its door opening, about 1.0 × 2.2 m, has a galvanised steel lintel and is being built down to size, and the shaft has a dark interior.
- **Courtyard / light well**, seen from the kitchen:
  - It is open to the sky, with tall walls around it, i.e. double height.
  - Teal (moisture-resistant) gypsum lines the soffit of the first-floor overhang.
  - Scaffolding stands in it, the ground is unfinished soil, and white downpipes run on the walls.
